# Supplements for Sprinters

Based on established sports-science consensus (AIS Sports Supplement Framework, IOC consensus
statements on dietary supplements). Not sourced from live web search — that tool wasn't
authorized for this run. Not medical advice; check with a doctor/dietitian first, especially if
you're a tested athlete (see contamination note below).

## Tier 1 — Strong evidence, worth taking

| Supplement | What it does for a sprinter | Typical dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | Increases phosphocreatine stores → more power in repeated short max-effort efforts (starts, reps) | 3–5 g/day maintenance (optional 20 g/day loading for 5–7 days) | Any time, daily, with consistency mattering more than timing |
| **Caffeine** | Improves reaction time, alertness, and perceived effort in short high-intensity efforts | 3–6 mg/kg bodyweight | 30–60 min pre-session/race |
| **Sodium bicarbonate** | Buffers acidosis, helps in repeated 200–400m-type efforts where lactate accumulates | 0.2–0.3 g/kg | 60–90 min pre-event; GI upset is common — test in training first, never race day |
| **Beta-alanine** | Raises muscle carnosine, buffers acid in max-effort efforts lasting 30s–a few minutes (most relevant for 200/400m) | 3.2–6.4 g/day, split doses | Daily for 4+ weeks to build up; causes tingling (paresthesia) — normal, not dangerous |

## Tier 2 — Solid but situational

| Supplement | When it's worth it |
|---|---|
| **Protein (whey/casein)** | Only if diet protein intake is below ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day. Food first — this fills a gap, doesn't add on top of an already sufficient diet. |
| **Vitamin D** | Only if blood levels are actually low (common in low-sunlight climates or indoor-heavy winters). Get tested, don't guess. |
| **Iron** | Only if bloodwork shows deficiency — common in female athletes and high-volume training blocks. Don't supplement blind; excess iron is harmful. |
| **Beetroot juice / nitrate** | Evidence is stronger for endurance than pure sprinting; marginal benefit for sprint-specific work. |

## Not worth it for sprint performance specifically

BCAAs, glutamine, most "pre-workout" proprietary blends, fat burners. Either no strong evidence
for sprint-specific power output, or the active ingredient (usually just caffeine) is cheaper
and cleaner bought alone.

## The one thing that matters more than any supplement

Sleep, total daily protein, and training load management move the needle more than anything on
this list. Supplements are a 2–5% edge on top of that foundation, not a substitute for it.

## Important caution if you compete under drug testing (WADA/USADA/national federation)

Supplement contamination is a real and common cause of positive tests — manufacturing facilities
often aren't segregated from banned-substance production. If you're a tested athlete:

- Only use products that are third-party batch-tested for sport (e.g., Informed Sport or NSF
  Certified for Sport certification on the specific batch, not just the brand).
- Creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, and sodium bicarbonate are not banned, but the products
  containing them can still be contaminated.
- When in doubt, don't — a positive test from a contaminated pre-workout has ended careers.
